Markets / 28 August 2026

Quality of market is more than market size.

A large market can support a small business. A quality market can support an enduring one.

Market size is an important input, but it is rarely the whole decision. The better question is whether the market rewards a company for solving a meaningful problem well—and whether those rewards become stronger as the company grows.

Four signals we look for.

Frequency. Problems that occur often create a stronger learning loop. A team sees the customer’s reality repeatedly and can improve with precision.

Consequence. When the problem affects revenue, risk, time or trust, customers can recognize the value of a better solution.

Change. Markets going through structural change create room for new systems, new habits and new leaders.

Durability. A strong market does not disappear when the first wave of attention moves elsewhere. It continues to reward a company that earns its position.

The work is in the nuance.

Good market analysis is not a single number. It is an understanding of how customers decide, how budgets move, where incumbents are strong and where the old way of working is becoming untenable.

For founders, that nuance can sharpen positioning. For investors, it can separate a temporary opening from a lasting opportunity.
